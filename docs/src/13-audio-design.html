01Audio Pillars

PillarIn practice
Sound tells the truthIn the dark, sound is the player's main sense. Every threat must be locatable by ear alone, so spirit sounds are always spatial, distinct and honest (no fake positions).
Silence is a soundQuiet stretches are planned. Insects and rain stopping is one of the strongest warnings in the game.
The world is aliveRain, frogs, wood and wind are dense, layered and specific to Cambodia, never generic "horror ambience".
Music is rareMost of the game has no music. When the Pinpeat tones arrive, they mean something.
Respect sacred soundChanting, bells and Pinpeat are used with permission and correct context, never distorted for cheap scares (section 6.3).

02The Mix

BusLevel guideRules
VoiceLoudestAlways intelligible; ducks ambience by 6 dB and music by 4 dB while speaking.
ThreatHighNever ducked. HRTF spatialisation for precise positioning.
SFXMediumPlayer's own sounds slightly quieter than threats so threats stand out.
AmbienceLow–mediumTwo to four layers per zone; crossfade over 2–3 s between zones.
MusicLowSits under everything; never masks a threat.
UILowPaper, pencil, satchel sounds; dry, non-spatial.
  • Loudness target: the overall mix averages around −18 LUFS on a test playthrough, with peaks below −1 dBTP. Dialogue sits around −20 to −18 LUFS.
  • Dynamic range: wide on purpose; horror needs quiet. A "night mode" setting compresses the range for players without headphones.
  • Headphones recommended: the start screen says so; "headphone mode" sharpens 3D positioning.

The silence rule

Before every major threat, the insect and frog layers fade out over 3–5 seconds. Players learn quickly that silence means danger, and the game can use that expectation later.

06Music Brief

6.1Direction

A sparse score built on traditional Khmer instruments, especially the roneat (រនាត) and the Pinpeat (ពិណពាទ្យ) ensemble, played slowly and quietly, with long silences, low drones and natural room sound. It should feel like music heard from far away across water at night.

6.3Working with musicians

  • Commission Cambodian musicians to record, or license existing recordings with clear written permission. Credit them by name.
  • Ask the musicians which pieces and modes are appropriate for funerals and which must not be used in a game.
  • Record instruments dry (close microphones) and with room sound, so they can be placed in the game's spaces.
  • Do not use real chanting or ceremonial music as a horror sting, and do not reverse or distort it.

07Voice Production

LanguageKhmer for all characters (Episode 1 Script, section 1); English and Khmer subtitles.
CastingSee the Episode 1 Script, section 2. Prefer actors from or familiar with Battambang for Yay Mey's accent.
RecordingQuiet, treated room; large-diaphragm or broadcast dynamic microphone; 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV; 20–30 cm from the mouth; pop filter.
TakesThree versions of each line (normal, quieter, more emotional) plus breaths and reactions (gasps, sighs) for Sora.
ProcessingLight noise reduction, gentle compression, high-pass at 80 Hz. Sok's recording gets a gramophone treatment (band-limited, crackle, slight wow and flutter).
Filesvo_<lineID>_t<take>.wav, e.g. vo_E1-A1-03-2_t2.wav.

Equipment: a portable recorder (for example a handheld stereo recorder) plus one dynamic and one condenser microphone is enough. Record at 48 kHz / 24-bit, several takes, with a few seconds of room tone each time.
