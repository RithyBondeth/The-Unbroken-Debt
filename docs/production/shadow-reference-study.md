# Shadow form: visual reference study

The current model's smooth torso, uniform hanging sleeves, simple skirt and thin
oval mask make it read as a mannequin. These references guide a replacement based
on sculpted anatomy and believable cloth. The observations and proposed changes
below are design judgments from inspecting the linked images.

## Primary reference: body, cloth and hands

Gilberto “Soren” Zaragoza — **Dementor fan art**:
https://www.artstation.com/artwork/XgBE0n

- Body view: https://cdna.artstation.com/p/assets/images/images/059/825/168/large/gilberto-soren-zaragoza-untitled-452.jpg?1677210928
- Detail view: https://cdnb.artstation.com/p/assets/images/images/059/825/113/large/gilberto-soren-zaragoza-untitled-451-copia.jpg?1677210937

Useful qualities: irregular layers of worn fabric, visible weave and thin torn
edges, gaunt underlying anatomy, a forward head posture, and fingers with distinct
knuckles and taper. The garment follows the shoulder and rib cage rather than
forming a smooth cone. Use these construction qualities as the main direction;
the project's Neak Ta retains its own restrained brass mask and identity.

## Supporting reference: posture and silhouette

Keith Thompson — **The Ritual movie designs**, original designer's published work:
http://blog.keiththompsonart.com/2025/01/the-ritual-movie-designs.html

- Posture study: https://keiththompsonart.com/images/full/parishioner01.jpg

Useful qualities: curved spine, a head carried forward of the chest, uneven limb
positions, and silhouettes that imply physical weight. Study the relationship of
neck, shoulders and arms. The film's antlers, body modifications and mythology
are not part of this character's design.

## Supporting reference: real metal mask

J. Paul Getty Museum — **Mask from a Cavalry Helmet**, bronze, A.D. 75–125:
https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/object/103SW8

- Photograph: https://media.getty.edu/iiif/image/e6fe50a1-b7a6-43d5-9e8b-941fe6779aba/full/2320,/0/default.jpg

Useful qualities: shaped eyelids and cheek planes, physical apertures and a formed
rim, uneven corrosion, small worn highlights and local changes in surface finish.
This is a material and construction reference, not a Khmer historical reference.
The Roman face, hair and ornament should not become the Neak Ta's iconography.

## Direction for the next model

1. Use an anatomical base for the shoulders, torso and hands; establish the
   threatening silhouette before texturing.
2. Author cloth over that anatomy with gravity, uneven layers and fabric tension;
   bake the folds into an efficient runtime mesh.
3. Sculpt an original, fitted brass mask with clear facial planes and socket
   depth; use restrained, varied wear instead of large round stains.
4. Show fragments of the figure in lamplight. Preserve enough contour and surface
   response for the materials to read in darkness.
5. Keep the existing encounter timings and navigation while replacing the art.

The linked artwork is reference material. It is not a game texture or a shipped
asset. The intended result is an original character consistent with the project's
Neak Ta art direction.

## Implemented village-ancestor direction

The user's subsequent four shrine photographs supersede the Dementor proposal above. The character now follows painted village ancestral statuary: anatomical aged male features, inset painted eyes, weathered grey-green mineral paint, a broad saffron cotton sash and earth-coloured wrap. The user also requested an angry face: the primary effigy has a lowered contracted brow, tightened jaw and downturned mouth. Original sculpture is shared by the forest apparition, hallway walker, under-house form and a pair of seated shrine ancestors. The shrine has a modest limewashed shelter, damp soot deposits, timber jambs and corrugated metal roofing; existing food, incense and leaf offerings remain in place. See `art-source/characters/ancestors/README.md` for sources and rebuilds.
