01Purpose and Principles

This game asks players around the world to experience Khmer belief and history. That is a privilege and a responsibility. This document keeps the representation accurate, respectful and reviewed.

PrincipleMeaning
Research before inventionStart from real practice and belief; invent only where the story needs it, and mark inventions clearly in the design documents.
Respectful horrorThe fear comes from the spirits and the debt, never from mocking or desecrating real religion.
Living cultureThese are beliefs and practices people hold today, not exotic decoration. Write as if Cambodian players are the first audience.
Cambodian voices decideWhen the advisors say something is wrong or hurtful, it changes.
Fiction is labelledThe specific pact, the seals and the rules of the debt are fiction. The game's credits or opening note can say so.

02Working with Advisors

WhoOne or two Cambodian advisors with knowledge of village religious practice (for example a family elder, a monk or lay temple leader, a university scholar of Khmer studies or a cultural organisation). A native-speaking translator for Khmer text.
WhenFor every episode: (1) story outline, (2) greybox with script, (3) release candidate. Plus ad-hoc questions while writing.
What to sendThe specific scenes, lines, images or items in question, the context, and a clear question. Avoid sending entire documents without guidance.
RecordingEvery question and answer goes in the review log (section 7) with the date and the decision.
Respect their timePay an honorarium where possible; credit advisors by name (or anonymously, if preferred); share the finished episode with them first.

03Topic Briefings

Each briefing summarises general understanding as a starting point for research. Nothing here is final until confirmed by an advisor.

TopicGeneral understandingHow the game uses it · to confirm
Neak Ta
អ្នកតា		Guardian spirits tied to a place (a village, a field, a forest, water). Often represented by a stone, a statue or a small shrine. People make offerings and ask for protection; Neak Ta are respected and can punish disrespect.A guardian enforcing a contract; a judge, not a demon. Confirm that a pact with a Neak Ta for prosperity is a plausible folk idea and how it should be described.
Funerals
បុណ្យសព		The family keeps the body at home for some days, with monks chanting, offerings and a vigil. Mourners wear white. The body is cremated. Ceremonies follow after seven days and after one hundred days.Episode 1's vigil, the white tent, the seventh night. Confirm vigil customs, white mourning dress, and the meaning of the seventh day.
Pchum Ben
ភ្ជុំបិណ្ឌ		A fifteen-day festival in the lunar month that usually falls in September or October. Families bring food to pagodas for the monks and rice balls for ancestors and wandering spirits, believed to be released at this time.Episode 2's night and the Pret. Confirm offering practices and whether lay people would be at the pagoda at night.
Pret
ប្រេត		In Buddhist belief, hungry ghosts suffering for past greed; often described with huge bellies and tiny mouths or thin necks, unable to eat.Blind hunters in Episode 2. Their blindness is our invention; confirm their appearance and that they can be shown as sympathetic.
BetelAreca nut, betel leaf and lime, traditionally offered to guests and used in rituals and offerings to ancestors and spirits.Episode 1's offering tray. Confirm the correct contents and arrangement.
Pagoda etiquetteShoes off in sacred buildings; respect for monks (women do not touch monks); Buddha images treated with great respect; specific behaviour around stupas holding ashes.Episode 2. The player must never be able to act disrespectfully towards monks or Buddha images.
Forest spirits
ខ្មោចព្រៃ		Folk belief in dangerous spirits in forests at night, and in the taboo against answering a voice that calls your name.Episode 4. Confirm the taboo's wording and region.
Drowned spiritsFolk belief in the spirits of those who die violently or in water, sometimes linked to women who die in childbirth.Episode 3 (Kanha). Highly sensitive; confirm terms and how to show her with dignity.
Colonial historyCambodia was a French protectorate from 1863. Rubber plantations expanded in the eastern provinces in the 1920s, with many labourers recruited from northern and central Vietnam under harsh conditions.Episode 4. Confirm with a historian; show labourers with dignity.
Mrenh Kongveal
ម្រេញគង្វាល		Small, child-like guardian spirits in Khmer folk belief, often described as protecting homes, fields or people.Kanha's baby, Pich, as a gentle helper (Ep 3, 5). Confirm that linking a stillborn child to a Mrenh Kongveal is appropriate, and how such spirits are spoken of.
Dance
របាំ		Khmer classical dance is treasured heritage, historically performed as an offering; village folk dances belong to festivals and harvests.Only as moments of beauty: a harvest dance in the Ep 2 flashback, Apsara carvings dancing in the Ep 5 Water trial. Check gestures and context with a dancer or the advisor.
Phnom Kulen
ភ្នំគូលេន		A sacred mountain north of Angkor with ancient carvings, waterfalls and pilgrimage sites; still an active place of worship.Episode 5. Keep the in-game sanctuary fictional; avoid copying specific real shrines.

04Language Guidelines

Khmer textWritten by native speakers, never machine-translated. Use Unicode Khmer and test rendering in the game engine (stacked consonants, vowel signs).
FontsPreah Vihear for interface and documents; a decorative display face for the logo, chosen with the advisor.
RomanisationUse common English spellings for places (Battambang, Kampong Cham, Tonle Sap). For other terms, choose one spelling, list it in the glossary and use it everywhere.
NamesKhmer names place the family name first. Characters are mostly addressed by given name with a kinship term.
Kinship termsYay (grandmother), Ta (grandfather), Pu (uncle), Bong (older sibling or cousin), Oun (younger). Confirm exact usage between Sora and Vuthy.
NumeralsKhmer numerals ០ ១ ២ ៣ ៤ ៥ ៦ ៧ ៨ ៩ appear in Episode 3's safe puzzle; the sketchbook shows a key.
