# Forest vegetation pass

The village uses the existing Cambodian banana grove, palms and bamboo as its
base. This pass adds a damp lowland broadleaf understory and a more enclosing
canopy. References guide plant structure and grouping; no reference photograph
is redistributed as a texture.

## References consulted

- [One Earth — Cardamom Mountains Rainforests](https://www.oneearth.org/ecoregions/cardamom-mountains-rainforests/).
  The Rhett Butler forest photograph and wet evergreen forest description inform
  the overlapping canopy, irregular trunk spacing and humid forest palette.
- [Mongabay — The ground layer of the rainforest](https://worldrainforests.com/05-rainforest-floor.html).
  Ground-layer and buttress-root reference discovered during the research pass.
- [Extraordinary Journeys — Cardamom Mountains](https://extraordinaryjourneys.com/destinations/asia/where-to-go/cardamom-mountains).
  Forest-trail photograph surfaced in image search: reference for enclosing
  vegetation around a narrow, readable walking route.

## Authored changes

- Broadleaf tree crowns and banyan foliage use individual folded leaves with
  pointed tips, branching veins, insect damage and a mix of green and dead leaves.
- Trunks have shallow fluting, solid buttress fins, moss-covered surface roots,
  climbing lianas and high hanging roots. Existing sacred cloth is preserved.
- Grass uses tapered, curved blades with ribbed normal maps, varied height,
  different bend directions and dry tips. Separate 8m patches allow culling.
- Fern rosettes, juvenile broad leaves and curled litter grow in groups along
  the grove. Sixteen additional secondary trees fill gaps beyond the walking route.
- Original texture maps are generated by `tools/areas/forest_detail.py` and stored
  in `art-source/textures/ep1/T_ep1_forest_*`. Existing CC0 bark is reused.

## Monsoon night sky and darker grade (26 October 2026)

The user found the sky poor and the night too bright. The painted sky dome
(`T_ep1_sky_storm`) was a flat, evenly lit image. `StormSky` now builds two
generated cloud maps (`stormClouds.ts`):

- A **high rain deck**, layered and stretched by the wind. It is lighter where the
  cloud thins and towards the horizon, so trees still stand dark against it. It is
  darkest overhead, and black below the horizon.
- **Low torn scud** near the horizon, which multiplies the deck so it reads as
  darker cloud in front.

The deck carries the light: a faint glow, plus lightning from inside the cloud
(`storm.level` raises it and adds a short pale fill). The scud is its base colour,
so it stays dark against a flash. Both drift round the dome at different speeds;
the scud runs about four times faster. It is a `StandardMaterial` with
emissive × diffuse, so the WebGPU pre-pass, volumetric fog and TAA are unchanged.

The night's light levels now live in `src/episodes/ep1/data/nightLook.ts` and are
darker:

| Setting | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base exposure | 1.85 | 1.45 |
| Adaptation cap | 2.8 | 2.25 |
| Night sight | 2.8 at 3.2 m | 2.0 at 2.8 m |
| Cloud bounce | 0.38 | 0.25 |
| Moon | 0.38 | 0.24 |
| Fog density | 0.028 | 0.031 |
| Fog ambient | (0.014, 0.019, 0.028) | (0.0075, 0.0105, 0.0165) |
| Clear colour | (0.02, 0.024, 0.032) | (0.007, 0.008, 0.012) |

The lamp, flames and lightning are unchanged, so lamplit areas stay readable.
Dawn uses `DAWN_LOOK`, with the same absolute values as before, so the resolution
still lifts. Unit tests (`tests/unit/stormSky.test.ts`) check the cloud maps'
orientation, wrap and structure, and that the night is darker and the dawn brighter.

## House surroundings and night readability

The follow-up adds ten broadleaf trees outside the compound: three on each
flank and four behind the house. Clustered ferns, juvenile leaves and sedge fill
the outer fence belt; a low planted strip follows the inside of the rear fence.
Trunks stay outside the fence and away from existing tree colliders, the pond,
well, shed entrance and walking routes. The full builder calls `house_forest()`;
`tools/areas/rebuild-house-forest.py` updates only this belt in existing scenes.

Night lighting now includes stronger cool cloud bounce, a modest moonlight
increase and less darkening of rain-soaked materials. Base exposure is 1.85 with
a dark-adaptation cap of 2.8, nearby night sight reaches 3.2m, and the fog has a faint blue-grey lift.
The grade retains its black point and warm lamplight. These settings apply in
normal play across the village acts; no preview-only light is needed to see the
vegetation. The lamp-out view remains dimmer than the lit view.

The forest review includes **House and forest**, **Behind the house** and a
**Player lamp** toggle for checking the shipped lighting with the lamp out.

Follow-up verification: all 254 tests pass. The focused belt adds 117 nodes
and 172,726 authored faces; its merge preserved 4,568 unrelated exported nodes.
The saved Blender scene was also audited after the concurrent house finish
rebuild and retains the added trees and undergrowth. Lamp-on and lamp-out views
were inspected with the overcast inspection light disabled; no browser runtime
errors were captured. Captures are `forest-house.png`, `forest-rear-lit.png`
and `forest-rear-unlit.png` in `debug-shots/`.

## Forest border and the end of the walk (26 October 2026)

The authored terrain stops 40 m either side of the path. Walking to it used to
show the world's edge and drop Sora off it. `ForestBorder`
(`src/episodes/ep1/act1/ForestBorder.ts`, layout in `src/episodes/ep1/data/forestBorder.ts`)
now builds the surroundings at load time, from copies of the village's own plants
taken before batching, so the village GLB does not grow:

- **Ground and road** continue beyond the terrain. They meet its edge height exactly,
  then rise slowly away. Raised bunds close the paddies east and west. The road bank
  and the telegraph line (poles and sagging wires) run on into the fog.
- **The walls' thicket.** Broadleaf, sugar palm, coconut, bamboo, dead trees, pachira,
  banana, shrubs and ferns are placed on jittered grids, each with its own turn and
  size. Invisible walls stand inside it at x = ±35, z = -35.5 and z = 88. On the
  road, a fallen tree in brush blocks each end.
- **Outer forest.** Modelled trees reach 14 m past the terrain (8 m on Low). Beyond
  them, crossed painted tree cards (`forestCards.ts`) reach 44 m (24 m on Low), up to
  the storm dome's painted horizon.
- **House belt.** This pass adds more canopy, a midstory of young trees, pachira,
  palms and bamboo, and a shrub and fern understory. It also adds standing puddles in
  hollows. The yard, path, pond, wells and every existing collider stay clear.
- **Fall safety net.** `FallGuard` (`src/player/FallGuard.ts`, run by `Game`) remembers
  the last solid footing. If Sora drops below y = -6, or ends up more than 1.5 m outside
  the walls, she is put back there behind a short fade.

Geometry is merged per material in 48 m cells for canopy, and 32 m cells for midstory
and understory (drawn within 42 m). Measured on the M1 Pro in the forest review page,
the added cost per view is:

| Preset | Draw calls | Triangles | CPU frame |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | +36 to +62 | +0.38 to +0.60 M | +0.5 to +1.5 ms |
| Low | +23 to +43 | +0.24 to +0.36 M | +0.4 to +0.7 ms |

The heaviest view (house) goes from 3.04 M to 3.60 M triangles on Medium and to
3.40 M on Low. Load adds 1.78 M triangles in 394 meshes on Medium (1.07 M on Low).
No integrated-GPU benchmark was possible on this machine.

Verified in the browser with the controller walking into each wall, both road ends,
the paddy bunds and a corner; the player stops at the walls and stays on the ground.
Dropping the player below the terrain or outside the walls returns her to her last
footing. The courtyard-walk regression in `encounters.html` still passes. Captures:
`debug-shots/before-*` and `after-*` (forest house/rear, each edge, road ends, Low).
Add `&quality=low` to the forest review URL to see the Low preset.

## The house weathered (26 October 2026)

The house is made older and more unsettling by additive geometry only. Nothing new
collides, and no gameplay or navigation node moves. `tools/areas/house_decay.py` is
called by the full builder after `house_details()`. `tools/areas/rebuild-house-decay.py`
updates just this pass (about 10,300 faces in 19 nodes); merge it with
`update-house-details-glb.mjs`. It adds:

- **Water damage:** streaks under every sill and from the top of each wall, mildew low
  on the boards, kitchen soot over both kitchen windows, and mud splashed up all 15
  stilts. These come from an original procedural atlas, `T_ep1_house_decay_BC`.
  They render as a cutout because this renderer's blended pass drops soft decals.
- **The roof:** termite tubes up two stilts, moss on the lower and north tiles,
  lichen higher up, and four gaps where tiles have slipped (with the battens showing).
  The slipped tiles lie lodged lower down the slope, and broken shards lie on the
  ground under both eaves.
- **Mourning:** soaked white mourning rags knotted to the rail and the stair post.
  (Thorny branches over the door and stair head were tried and removed at the
  user's request.)
- **Neglect:** two paper wasp nests under the eaves, slatted bamboo fish traps (lop)
  hung from the girders above head height, and five more silk webs.

The posts, beams and trim (`M_house_posts`, 339 objects) change from a glossy
reddish varnished grain to the weathered teak maps the gate posts already used, with
the checks running along each post. `retexture-house-posts.py` changes the Blender
material in place, and `update-house-posts-glb.mjs` points the shipped material at
the textures already in the GLB, so the download does not grow for it. Captures:
`debug-shots/before-house-*` and `after-house-*`.

## Rebuilding

The full village builder calls the same shared geometry functions. For a focused
pass, use the commands in `tools/areas/rebuild-forest.py`, then compress its export
and merge it using `tools/areas/update-forest-glb.mjs`. The adjacent JSON manifest
identifies exactly which nodes are replaced. Keep a backup of the current village
before merging. Do not overwrite concurrent prop work with a full-world export.

The Blender rebuild asserts unrelated object transforms, mesh references and
custom properties, required gameplay anchors and new trunk clearance. The merge
also checks the rest of the shipped GLB remains intact.

## Visual review

Open `/tests/browser/forest.html?start=act1`. It starts silently, uses isolated
save storage and renders the actual village with the production lamp, fog and
colour grade. The toolbar covers the path, grass, trunk roots, canopy, road and
compound edge; **Save screenshot** writes `debug-shots/forest-<view>.png`.
**Overcast detail light** adds an optional neutral fill for inspecting geometry
and surfaces. It is exclusive to this development fixture; the default view uses
the shipped night lighting.

Review on target hardware: replacing cards with curved geometry increases
triangle count. Small patches and coarse material batching keep distant foliage
cullable, but the browser review is not a guarantee of performance on every GPU.
