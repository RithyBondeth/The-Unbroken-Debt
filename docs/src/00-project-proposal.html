01Executive Summary

Boon Sop: The Unbroken Debt (បុណ្យសព៖ បំណុលមិនដាច់) is a first-person atmospheric survival horror game that runs directly in desktop web browsers. It is set in Cambodia in 1932, during the French Protectorate, and draws on Khmer folklore, Buddhist ritual and colonial history.

The player is Sora, a 24-year-old illustrator from Phnom Penh. She comes home to Battambang for the funeral of her Uncle Sok and learns that in 1910 her great-grandfather made a pact with a Neak Ta (អ្នកតា), a guardian spirit of the land: prosperity for the family in exchange for one life from each of four generations. Three lives have been paid. The fourth falls due on the seventh night after Sok's death, and Sora discovers that the name written for that final payment is her own.

Across five episodes and seven nights she must break the five seals of the pact, uncover what her uncle really did, and decide what to do about the cousin who would rather she paid.

Why this game, why now

An unused settingHorror games regularly draw on Western, Japanese and Korean folklore. Khmer folklore and 1930s Cambodia are almost absent from games.
No-friction accessWebGPU now brings near-native 3D rendering to the browser on gaming PCs and Apple Silicon Macs. A link is easier to share than a store page, with no installer.
Tight, episodic and achievableShort, dense episodes respect players' time and keep the project achievable for a solo developer. Episode 1 tests the idea with real players before the rest is built.

02Vision and Design Pillars

The player should feel like a stranger in her own family's history: alone in the dark, carrying a small light, running out of nights, and surviving through knowledge of rituals and taboos rather than through force.

2.1Design pillars

PillarWhat it meansHow it shows up in play
Cultural authenticityKhmer architecture, ritual, music and belief are the foundation of the horror, not decoration.Researched and advisor-reviewed settings; rituals and offerings solve problems.
Light is lifeSora's kerosene lamp is her only defence and her biggest risk.Oil runs out, light draws some spirits, and shutters and matches force constant choices.
Fully diegetic interfaceNo on-screen HUD.Map, notes, inventory and the night count live in Sora's satchel and sketchbook.
Knowledge over forceSora never fights.Stealth, sound, line of sight, taboos and counter-rituals replace combat.
Every night is differentNo two episodes play the same way.A new place, spirit and mechanic each episode, plus a structural surprise: a daylight market, a playable flashback, a companion.
Respect is rewardedThe spirit judges how Sora treats the dead, the living and the truth.Offerings, taboos, compassion and honesty feed directly into the ending.

2.2Pacing

Each episode is short and dense: roughly 5–6 puzzles, 3–4 escalating threat encounters, one optional side area and one key choice in about two hours. Every act introduces something new, and every episode ends with a revelation that changes what the player thinks is happening.

03Story

3.1The debt

In 1910, famine and cholera swept a forest village in Kampong Cham. Sora's great-grandfather Chea went to the village's Neak Ta shrine and offered the family's silver betel box, pleading for his children's lives and for land and fortune. The guardian accepted. The price was one life from each of four generations, the fourth falling due one full twelve-year cycle after the third. The pact was bound by five consecrated objects, the seals.

The family survived and prospered, moving to rich rice land in Battambang. The guardian collected on schedule. In the 1920s a French rubber company cleared the forest and built its factory over the forgotten shrine.

3.2The family

GenerationMemberFate
1Chea ជា · great-grandfatherMade the pact in 1910. First payment: taken days later.

4Sora សូរ៉ា · 24The player. Stopped answering her grandmother's letters after 1929 and never came home. The name now written for the fourth payment.

3.3Series synopsis

In 1932 the fourth payment falls due. Sok, desperate to save his son, secretly performs a ritual in 1931 that rewrites the debtor's name from Vuthy to Sora. Riddled with guilt, he then tries to break the seals himself, but only the named debtor can break them, and on Night 1 the guardian strikes him dead. His journal, addressed to "whoever keeps my vigil", is all he leaves behind.

Sora follows the journal, believing she is ending a curse on the family. In Episode 3 she lives the truth through her uncle's memory: he chose her to die in his son's place, and the drowned spirit haunting the clinic is her own mother. Sok gave her the burden, and also the only way out: if the named debtor breaks all five seals and performs the Ritual of Severance, the debt ends and no one else has to die.

Ending the pact has a cost. "The guardian takes back what it gave." The family's land and fortune would return to the earth. And Vuthy, told by Yay Mey that his was the first name written, believes that if Sora tries and fails, the guardian will come for him instead. He would rather she simply paid.

The series is about what we owe the land and each other. Chea, the colonial company, Sok and Vuthy all take without paying; Sora, who ran from her family, must decide whether to give back. The full theme, rules of the debt and character arcs are in the Story Bible.

3.4The seven nights

The game takes place over seven nights in the monsoon season of 1932, during the fortnight of Pchum Ben. The guardian collects on the seventh night after Sok's death.

NightEpisodeWhat happens
1Prologue (Episode 1)Sok dies at 8:12 PM trying to burn the first seal. Yay Mey telephones Sora.
31 · The House of MourningSora keeps the funeral vigil and breaks Seal #1.
42 · The Whispering PagodaThe last night of Pchum Ben. Seal #2.
53 · The Drowned VillageThe truth about Sok and Kanha. Seal #3.
64 · The Rubber PlantationThe shrine under the factory; Vuthy's betrayal. Seal #4.
75 · The Sunken SanctuaryThe Ritual of Severance before dawn.

3.5The antagonist: the Neak Ta

The Neak Ta appears as a towering shadow wearing an ancient brass mask, growing stronger as each seal breaks. In Khmer belief, Neak Ta are respected guardians of the land, and this one is not evil: it is enforcing a contract the family entered willingly and has tried to cheat twice. It watches how Sora treats the dead, the living and the truth. The true ending reflects this: the guardian bows and releases the family rather than being destroyed.

The game keeps its spirits few and distinct. The hostile spirits are the Neak Ta (every shadow and moving statue is one of its forms), the Pret and the Khmaoch Prey. Two of the family's own dead also appear: Sora's mother Kanha, a bound and grieving soul, and her baby son Pich, a gentle guardian spirit who helps Sora.

4.1Episode summaries

Ep 1 · The House of MourningSora keeps the night vigil over Uncle Sok's coffin: lamp fuelled, incense burning, protective thread unbroken. His journal reveals the pact and the seven-night deadline. She burns the Bronze Token before 3:00 AM, then finds a slip of paper in Sok's hand with her own name beside the words "the fourth".
Ep 2 · The Whispering PagodaOn the last night of Pchum Ben, blind hungry ghosts hunt by sound among the stupas. In the scripture library, Sora relives the 1910 pact as Chea himself. At the 4:00 AM bell she returns the betel box to her ancestors' stupa and the seal breaks.
Ep 3 · The Drowned VillageA daylight floating market gives way to a monsoon night. In the abandoned clinic, Sora learns that Sok renamed the debt to spare his son, and that the water spirit is her mother. Someone has cut her boat loose.
Ep 4 · The Rubber PlantationBy telephone, Yay Mey confesses she knew. Vuthy arrives to "help". In the fog, the mimics speak with his voice too. The factory stands on the 1910 shrine. Vuthy tries to stop the seal from breaking and is seized by the guardian. Sora must choose whether to save him.
Ep 5 · The Sunken SanctuaryBeneath Phnom Kulen, four trials each end with a memory of one generation of the debt. At the altar, Sora performs the Ritual of Severance while the guardian attacks, and decides Vuthy's fate.

05Core Gameplay

5.1Core loop

ExploreSearch the location by lamplight
DiscoverNotes, items and clues; the sketchbook records them
SurviveAvoid spirits using darkness, sound, hiding and taboos
SolveGrounded puzzles built on Khmer custom and 1930s technology
RitualBreak the episode's seal, then face a revelation

5.2Key systems

SystemDescription
Light and resourcesKerosene lamp, limited matches, and later a shutter that hides the light without putting out the flame.
Diegetic sketchbookSora draws maps, copies inscriptions, writes notes and marks the nights remaining.
Stealth and perceptionSpirits sense by sight, sound or both. Surfaces change footstep noise; crouching, breath and distractions help.
EscalationEach episode's spirit grows more dangerous act by act: more of them, sharper senses, new ways to reach Sora.
Cultural taboosRules from Khmer belief, such as never answering a voice calling your name in the forest at night. Breaking them has consequences now and at the end.
PuzzlesPhysical and cultural: a betel offering tray, temple bells, Khmer lunar dates, a French–Khmer cipher, mirrors, fuses, gears.
Optional areasOne per episode, holding lore notes that teach the chants needed in the finale.
ChoicesOne key choice per episode, about honesty, compassion and Vuthy.

8.1System requirements

MinimumNVIDIA RTX 2060 / AMD RX 5700 / Apple M1 Pro · 16 GB RAM · 1080p
RecommendedNVIDIA RTX 3070 / AMD RX 6800 / Apple M2 Pro or better · 16–32 GB RAM · 1440p
BrowserA WebGPU browser: current Chrome or Edge; Safari on macOS 26+; Firefox on Windows or Apple Silicon

The game targets high-end desktop hardware and requires WebGPU; there is no WebGL2 fallback. A quick compatibility check runs before play, and players on unsupported browsers or hardware see a clear requirements page. Laptops with integrated graphics and mobile devices are not supported.

09Production Plan

A solo developer builds the game, using licensed libraries for generic assets and custom work for everything culturally specific. Episode 1 is built and released first.

PhaseNameLengthMain outcome

That is about 35 weeks to the Episode 1 release, then about 4 months per later episode, for a total of roughly 2–2.5 years at about 25 hours a week. Details are in the Development Plan.

11Conclusion and Next Steps

Boon Sop: The Unbroken Debt brings Khmer folklore to a global horror audience through the most accessible platform there is. Its seven-night countdown, betrayal at the heart of the family and choice-driven endings give a short series real weight.

  1. Review this version of the story with one or two Cambodian cultural advisors.
  2. Write the Episode 1 beat sheet and a one-page art bible.
  3. Install the toolchain and run the technical spikes.
  4. Build the Episode 1 greybox and playtest it.
