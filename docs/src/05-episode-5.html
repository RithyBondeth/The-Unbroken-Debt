01Episode Overview

The fused blood line leads Sora to Phnom Kulen, the sacred mountain. Behind a waterfall lies a sunken temple where the Neak Ta keeps the original contract. Everything she has learned is tested in four elemental trials before she faces the guardian itself and performs the ritual that will either free her family or bind her forever.

1.1Episode goal

Place the four cleansed seal remnants on their elemental shrines, open the Sanctum of the Banyan Tree, and complete the Ritual of Severance on the Golden Inscription Slab to end the ancestral debt.

1.2Role in the series

IntroducesA hub with trials playable in any order, the chant choice interface, a three-phase boss ritual and the ending resolver.
ReusesEvery earlier skill: watched/unwatched (Episode 1), mirror beams (Episode 2), sound stealth (Episode 2), resource timing (Episode 1), and the white thread and incense rituals.
ConcludesThe story of the debt, with two endings decided by the player's actions across all five episodes.

1.3Visual identity

Ancient mossy sandstone, glowing spirit amber, deep jungle green, golden runes that glow on their own, drifting mist, and a dramatic final change to warm golden morning light.

04Story and Gameplay by Act

Act I · The Mountain Path and the Four Gateways

Playtime 0:00 – 1:00 · Story 3:00 – 3:45 AM

Narrative

At 3:00 AM, Sora reaches Phnom Kulen (ភ្នំគូលេន). Guided by her sketchbook and the molten ingot, she climbs the ancient Naga staircase through tropical mist and enters a hidden cave behind a roaring waterfall.

Inside lies the Central Rotunda of the Four Elements. A stone altar shows four empty niches, one for each seal she broke in Episodes 1–4. To open the way to the Final Sanctum, she must enter the four elemental chambers and place each cleansed seal remnant on its matching shrine.

Gameplay

ElementDescription
Hub navigationThe four trials can be played in any order from the rotunda.
Placing the remnantsSora takes each cleansed remnant from her satchel (Bronze Token fragment, Silver Betel Box inlay, Terracotta Jar soil, molten iron ingot) and places it on its elemental altar.

Act III · The Ritual of Severance

Playtime 2:30 – 4:00 · Story 4:45 AM – sunrise

Narrative

With all four shrines active, the heavy stone doors of the rotunda slide open to reveal the Sanctum of the Banyan Tree.

At its centre lies the Golden Inscription Slab, the fifth and final seal, bearing the original blood contract of 1910. As Sora approaches, the ground trembles and the Neak Ta Guardian manifests in its full, terrifying form: a multi-armed being of shadow and ancient stone, towering over the altar.

Sora cannot fight it. She must complete the Ritual of Severance while evading its attacks (see section 5).

05The Ritual of Severance (Boss Sequence)

The ritual has three phases. Throughout, the Guardian attacks the area with sweeping blows and reaches for Sora with shadow tendrils. Each phase starts from its own checkpoint.

PhasePlayer actionsPressure and failure
1 · The Four CornersRun to the four corner pillars of the sanctum, tie blessed white cotton thread around each, and light sanctified incense at each.Area attacks and tendrils between pillars. Being hit costs time and causes shadow damage.
2 · The ChantsAt the central altar, recite the counter-mantras by choosing lines that match the funeral chants (ស្មូត) recorded in the sketchbook.Each wrong choice causes shadow damage and is counted toward the ending.
3 · The Blood OfferingPrick a finger with a brass pin and place a drop of blood on the Golden Inscription Slab to formally nullify the ancestral debt.Final, uninterrupted moment; the outcome depends on the ending conditions.
Shadow damage

There is no health bar. Damage is shown by the screen darkening, sound distortion and Sora's breathing. After two or three hits (target) Sora is overwhelmed and the phase restarts from its checkpoint.

06Endings

True ending · The Unbroken Freedom

Conditions: the player kept the cultural taboos, collected all hidden lore notes, and completed the ritual chants without error.

Resolution: As Sora places her blood on the slab and recites the final chant, a blinding golden light bursts from the banyan tree. The Neak Ta lowers its hands, bows its masked head in respect and dissolves into glowing dust. Above the open dome, the night sky turns into a golden sunrise. Sora walks out of the catacombs into the morning sun, free from the past. Back in Battambang, Uncle Sok's cremation fire burns peacefully under a clear sky as her family finds peace.

Bad ending · Bound to the Soil

Conditions: the player made major errors in the chants or failed key lore choices during the series.

Resolution: Sora completes the ritual, but the counter-mantra is incomplete. The Neak Ta rejects the severance and declares the debt unpaid. Darkness swallows the sanctum. Sora wakes inside the wooden coffin at the Battambang stilt house from Episode 1, trapped forever in the spirit world, while her family mourns over her lifeless body in the world of the living.

11Revision Notes and Open Issues

Changes from v1.0

  • Reorganised into the standard episode structure; separated the boss sequence and endings into their own sections; added ending-condition tracking, mechanics, item and glossary sections.
  • Defined shadow damage (no health bar; two or three hits restart the phase).
  • Khmer text now renders correctly.

Open issues

Decide before building Episode 5
  • "Four artifacts she destroyed": the Silver Betel Box is never destroyed or cleansed in Episode 2. Add that ritual to Episode 2 so the "inlay" remnant exists.
  • "Key lore choices" are not defined in any earlier episode. Design one or two meaningful choices per episode, or remove this condition.
  • Which remnant belongs to which element (for example earth = token, water = jar soil, fire = ingot, wind = betel box inlay).
  • Pret in the Wind trial: explain in the sketchbook why they appear here.
  • The blood offering and the linga/yoni altar are sensitive; review with the cultural advisor and keep the presentation restrained.
  • The Guardian's mask name ("Mukhalingi") needs replacing with an accurate term.
