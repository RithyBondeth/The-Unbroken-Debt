01Goals

QuestionHow we find out
Is it scary?Observed reactions, self-reported scares, where players hesitate or retreat
Is it clear?Where players get lost or stuck; whether they can explain the story afterwards
Is it the right length?Time per act against the beat sheet targets (1 h 35 – 1 h 55 total)
Is it fair?Deaths per area, causes of deaths, frustration moments
Does it run?Frame rate, loading time and crashes on testers' own computers and browsers
Is it respectful and accurate?Sessions with Cambodian players and the cultural advisor

02Test Rounds

RoundBuildTestersFocus
R0 · SelfEvery weekYouPlay the whole episode start to finish; note anything broken or boring.
R1 · GreyboxEnd of Phase 23–5Pacing, clarity, length, whether it is scary with placeholder shapes. Complete the greybox checklist (beat sheet section 8).
R2 · SystemsEnd of Phase 33–5Controls, the satchel and sketchbook, lamp and resources, AI fairness, checkpoints.
R3 · AlphaMid Phase 45–8Atmosphere, sound, readability in the dark, performance on varied hardware.
R4 · Release candidatePhase 58–12Final polish, bugs, browser coverage, cultural review, first impressions.

Use different testers in each round where possible: fresh eyes reveal confusion that returning testers no longer notice.

03Recruiting Testers

GroupWhyShare of testers
Horror game playersThe core audience; they know the genre's conventionsAbout half
Occasional playersReveal control and clarity problemsAbout a quarter
Cambodian playersCultural accuracy, language, emotional resonanceAt least two per round from R3
Different hardwareIntegrated-graphics laptops, Macs, older PCs; Chrome, Edge, Firefox, SafariMix across all groups
  • Friends of friends are better than close friends, who tend to be kind rather than honest.
  • Communities: local game-developer groups, university game clubs, Cambodian student associations, horror-game forums (for remote tests).
  • Ask for consent to observe or record, explain what is collected, and let testers stop at any time. Warn testers that the game contains horror themes and depictions of death and grief.

04Running a Session

StepTimeWhat to do
1 · Welcome5 minExplain that you are testing the game, not them; there are no wrong answers; consent and content warning; headphones on.
2 · Play90–120 minSay nothing and do not help. Observe and take notes on the observer sheet. Only step in if they are stuck for more than 5 minutes; note it.
3 · Questionnaire10 minThe questions in section 7, filled in alone before talking.
4 · Interview10–15 minAsk them to retell the story; ask about the scariest moment, the most confusing moment, and what they would change.
5 · Thank—Credit testers in the game if they wish.
Remote sessions

Testers play the web build at home while sharing their screen and webcam in a video call, or send a recording afterwards. The playtest log (section 6) fills in what you cannot see.

06Playtest Logging

Development and playtest builds keep a local event log that testers can export at the end with one button. It is not sent anywhere automatically.

LoggedDetail
Beats and checkpointsTime each beat starts (P01…R05), so time per act is exact
CatchesWhich entity, where (position) and when
HintsWhich control hints appeared, and how long before the action was performed
ResourcesOil level over time, matches used, times the lamp ran dry
Optional contentShed opened, lore notes found, choice made at dawn
PerformanceFPS once per second, area load times, browser, graphics API (WebGPU or WebGL2), GPU name if available

No personal data is recorded. Release builds disable the log unless the player turns it on to report a bug.

09Turning Feedback into Changes

SeverityDefinitionAction
CriticalBlocks progress, crashes, or culturally harmfulFix before the next round
HighMost testers confused, stuck or frustrated at the same pointFix before the next round
MediumSome testers affected; pacing or clarity issueFix if time allows; retest
LowOne tester's preferenceNote; act if it recurs
  • Watch what players do more than what they say. If three people walk past the shed, the shed needs a better hint, even if nobody mentions it.
  • Look for patterns. One person stuck is a data point; three people stuck in the same place is a design problem.
  • Fix the cause, not the symptom. If players miss the drawer key, improve the gramophone clue rather than adding an arrow.
  • After each round, write a one-page summary: top five problems, decisions made, and what to retest.
