02Scope and Roadmap

2.1The honest size of the project

The original proposal budgeted 11 months for a five-person team, roughly 55 person-months. With the shorter, denser episodes chosen in v3.0 (8–10 hours in total instead of 15–20), a solo developer should expect about 2–2.5 years at 25 hours per week. To keep it achievable:

  1. Ship Episode 1 alone first, as a free or pay-what-you-want release, to test the idea with real players.
  2. Build every system once in Episode 1 and reuse it in Episodes 2–5.
  3. Cut cost, not atmosphere. Darkness, fog, sound and first-person framing hide a lot.

Estimates assume about 25 focused hours per week. Scale them to your real availability.

Phase 2 · Episode 1 greybox

4 weeks

Build the whole episode from grey boxes: no final art, but every room at the right scale and every trigger, puzzle, enemy path, line of text and the dawn key choice in place. Target: 1.5–2 hours for a first-time player.

Exit: a friend can play Episode 1 start to finish, and it is scary even with boxes. If it isn't, fix the pacing now; art will not save it.

7 weeks

Turn the spike code into reusable systems (section 4): interaction, inventory, sketchbook, lamp and resources, stealth perception, entity AI, scripted events, saves, settings and accessibility. Add automated tests for game logic.

2.4Solo cut list

If time runs short, cut from the top. None of these damages the core experience.

  1. Spoken dialogue → written text, with Khmer voice for key lines only.
  2. Full-body characters → Sora is hands-only; NPCs mostly seated, still or in silhouette.
  3. Many unique props → fewer props, varied with material tints and decals.
  4. Physics-driven debris in escapes → scripted animation.
  5. Rowboat physics (Episode 3) → boat on a path with limited steering.
  6. Daylight market crowd (Episode 3) → a few static vendors and ambient sound.
Avoid as a solo developer
  • Building a level editor: use Blender as the level editor and export metadata with custom properties.
  • Writing engine features Babylon.js already has (physics, audio, picking, GUI, post-processing).
  • Starting Episode 2 content before Episode 1 is released.
  • Polishing art in a room whose gameplay is not final.

3.3Core architecture

Principle: systems are generic; episodes are data plus a few scripts.

PartHow it works
Event busSystems never call each other directly. The lamp announces lamp:extinguished; the AI listens and lowers visibility. This keeps systems reusable.
Game flagsOne key-value store holds all story progress (for example ep1.gramophone.solved). Saves serialise it; Episode 5 reads flags from Episodes 1–4.
Scripted sequencesWritten as readable async functions (walk to, play sound, wait for look), so no visual scripting tool is needed.
State machinesEntities and puzzles use small explicit state machines (idle → patrol → investigate → chase → lose track) in plain TypeScript.
Blender as level editorTriggers, spawn points, patrol nodes, hiding spots and audio zones are empties with custom properties, exported to glTF and turned into game objects by the loader.
Area streamingEach episode is split into areas loaded and unloaded as asset containers. Shared assets stay loaded; unloaded areas dispose their meshes, materials and textures.

3.4Rendering

FeatureApproach
Lamp lightOne point light with shadows; usually the only dynamic shadow caster.
Candles and small flamesUnshadowed point lights with flicker, emissive meshes and glow; test Babylon 9's clustered lighting in spike S2.
Static lightingBaked lightmaps or ambient occlusion from Blender for architecture.
FogScene fog plus animated fog cards near the player; a volumetric shader only if it fits the budget (important for Episode 4).
Post-processingGrain, chromatic aberration, vignette, bloom and colour grading (a sepia look for Episode 1).
Special materialsNode Material Editor for the entity shadow, wet surfaces, blood on the map and glowing runes.
Quality presetsLow / Medium / High, chosen automatically from a quick benchmark on first launch.

3.6Save system

What is savedEpisode, area, checkpoint, flags, inventory, sketchbook entries, resource levels, settings and ending counters (taboos broken, lore notes found).
WhenFixed checkpoints and episode completion; no free saving, which keeps the tension.
WhereIndexedDB, three slots, versioned, with migrations for future changes.
Save codesJSON → compressed → URL-safe base64 with a checksum, for moving progress between devices and into the next episode.
Security noteBrowser-side encryption cannot stop a determined player because the key ships with the game. Save codes are tamper-evident, not secret, which is fine for a single-player game.

3.8Input and accessibility

  • Keyboard and mouse first; gamepad support through the Gamepad API.
  • Rebindable keys, mouse sensitivity, invert Y, field-of-view slider.
  • Subtitles and captions for important sounds, with a visual-cue option for deaf and hard-of-hearing players.
  • Toggles for head-bob, film grain and chromatic aberration (motion sickness and comfort).
  • Brightness calibration screen.
  • English and Khmer text from the start, with all strings stored in data files.

4.2Fail state

The episode specifications never said what happens when Sora fails. Recommended:

  • Caught → short scare, fade to black, a page torn from the sketchbook, restart at the last checkpoint.
  • No health bar. Episode 5's shadow damage is shown by darkening and sound, with two or three hits before failure.
  • Running out of oil doesn't kill; it makes the dark more dangerous.
