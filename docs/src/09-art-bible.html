01Visual Pillars

PillarWhat it means in practice
A 1930s photograph come to lifeWarm sepia mid-tones, soft grain, gentle vignetting, slightly lifted blacks in daylight and deep blacks at night. Nothing looks digitally clean.
Warm flame against cold darkEvery night scene is built from two temperatures: amber light from flames and blue-grey from the moon or the dark. The player's lamp is always the warmest thing on screen.
Darkness is a materialDarkness is designed, not just absent light. Silhouettes must still read at the edge of the lamp; true black is reserved for places where something may be hiding.
Grounded and lived-inRealistic proportions, worn surfaces, objects that have been used for decades. Exaggeration is reserved for the spirits.
Respect for the realKhmer architecture, clothing and sacred objects are depicted accurately and with dignity (section 11).

2.3Colour rules

  • Saturation is earned. Most surfaces stay below about 40% saturation. Strong colour is reserved for flame, blood, brass, sacred red and gold, and the daylight market.
  • Blood is dark and brownish (#5E1A14), never bright red, and it looks like ink on paper.
  • Memories and flashbacks shift to warm sepia with lower contrast so the player always knows they are in the past.

03Lighting

LightColourRules
Player lamp (open)#FF9A3C ~1900 KThe hero light: high-resolution soft shadows. Useful light 6–8 m; falls off fast beyond. Flicker: intensity ±12% with smooth noise at 6–10 Hz; stronger flicker when oil is low or Sora is cold.
Player lamp (shuttered)SameLeaks a thin line of light (0.5 m) through the shutter slits; the player can still see their hands.
Candles, oil lamps, incense#FFB15EClustered point lights, radius 2–4 m, with emissive flame cards and bloom; up to 4 nearby flames may cast shadows in key moments. Flicker out of phase with each other.
Moonlight#8FA8D8Low intensity; one directional light; used for silhouettes, not for seeing detail.
Lightning#DFE8FFVery short (80–150 ms) bursts that briefly reveal the space; schedule them, never use them as a cheap jump scare more than once per area.
Daylight (Ep 3 market)Warm afternoonThe only daylight scene in the night episodes. Bright, saturated, lively, to make the coming night darker by contrast.
Baked lighting—Static architecture uses lightmaps baked in Blender, layered with real-time SSAO, contact shadows and screen-space reflections on wet surfaces.
Darkness rule

A player standing in the dark with the lamp off should see faint shapes within about 2 m after a few seconds (simulated eye adjustment). Pure, unreadable black is used only for doorways, the space under furniture and the backs of rooms, where the player must imagine what is there.

05Scale, Geometry and Modular Kits

5.1Units and grid

  • 1 Blender unit = 1 metre. Z is up in Blender; the glTF exporter converts to Y-up.
  • Modular pieces snap to a 0.5 m grid; pivots at the bottom-left corner of each piece.
  • Door openings 0.9 × 2.0 m (low, to fit traditional houses); ceiling 2.7 m; stair step 0.2 m rise, 0.25 m tread.

06Materials and Textures

RuleDetail
WorkflowPBR metal/roughness (glTF standard): base colour, normal, and a packed occlusion-roughness-metallic map.
Texel densityEnvironment 1,024 px/m · hero props 2,048 px/m · player items 4,096 px/m equivalent.
Maximum sizes4096² for hero assets, player items and trim sheets · 2048² for props · 1024² for small props. All shipped as KTX2.
Trim sheetsOne teak trim sheet (planks, beams, carved edges) and one plaster/stucco trim sheet cover most architecture.
WearEvery surface shows age: worn edges, grime in corners, water marks at the base of walls and posts.
WetnessA shader parameter darkens base colour and lowers roughness; used for rain, mud and flooded rooms.
Library assetsAlways retexture or tint library assets to the episode palette and add wear before use.

09Spirits and Creatures

SpiritDesign languageRules
Neak TaSilhouette first: a towering shape of layered shadow, like smoke and stone. The brass mask is the only detailed element: hammered, ancient, slightly asymmetrical, with deep eye sockets that catch the lamp. In Episode 5: multiple arms, stone texture showing through the shadow.Never fully lit before Episode 5. Never copy a real deity's iconography. Dignified, not grotesque: a judge.
Neak Ta shadow formsThe guardian's lesser forms: tall, stooped, wet shadows in the house (Ep 1) and the manor (Ep 4); weathered statues in the Earth trial (Ep 5). They share the mask's brass glint in the eyes so players recognise one spirit.Mostly shown through shadows and sound.
Pich (Mrenh Kongveal)Never fully seen: a small silhouette at the edge of the light, a child's wet handprint on a dry board, a little wooden toy that rolls. Warm-toned, never pale or distorted.Gentle; never used for a scare.

11Sensitivity Rules

Always
  • Show Buddha images, monks, stupas and altars with respect: well lit, intact, never as monsters or jump scares.
  • Base clothing, architecture and ritual objects on research and advisor review.
  • Show the plantation labourers as people with names and dignity.
  • Let the spirits be frightening and sympathetic.
  • Show classical and village dance as beauty, joy and offering, never as ghosts or monsters.
Never
  • Damage, desecrate or mock sacred images or objects.
  • Copy the iconography of a specific real deity or a real Neak Ta shrine.
  • Use gore for shock; blood stays small and symbolic.
  • Sexualise any character, living or dead.
