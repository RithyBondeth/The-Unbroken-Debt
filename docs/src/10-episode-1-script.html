01Conventions and Language

Line IDsE1-<beat>-<n>, for example E1-A1-03-2. The beat matches the beat sheet.
Spoken languageAll characters speak Khmer, recorded by Khmer-speaking actors. The French carriage driver line is the only exception (French-accented Khmer is also acceptable). English and Khmer subtitles.
Written languageIn-world documents are written in Khmer (Sok's journal, the deed) or French (official papers). The player reads them in their chosen language; the sketchbook keeps Sora's summary.
Master textEnglish below is the master for meaning and timing. The Khmer script must be written by a native speaker, not machine-translated, and reviewed by the cultural advisor.
Sora's voiceSparse. She speaks only when it matters: under her breath, dry, controlled. Most of her thoughts appear in the sketchbook instead.

04Sok's Recording

Heard on the gramophone in beat A2-05, once the speed is corrected. About 55 seconds. Hoarse, urgent, close to the microphone, with gramophone crackle.

E1-A2-05 · Uncle Sok · gramophone record If this is playing, then I am dead, and you are the one keeping my vigil. Listen to me.

In 1910 my grandfather, Chea, made a bargain with the guardian of the land. Our lives, and a fortune, for a price: one of us from each generation. Four lives. Three have been paid. My father. My sister.

On the seventh night after my death, it will come to collect what is owed. Break the seals before then. There are five. The first is in my chest. Its key I gave to the water jars below the house. The key to my desk is where the ancestors keep watch.

I tried to begin. If you are hearing this, I failed. Forgive me. Forgive me for all of it.

Sok says "my sister" but never names who the fourth payment is. That silence is deliberate.

05Sok's Journal (required pages)

Journal page A · behind the loose plank (A2-03) To whoever keeps my vigil.

My grandfather Chea went to the guardian's shrine in the forest in the famine year, with our family's silver betel box in his hands. The village was dying. His children were dying. He came home with the box full of earth and a promise.

We were saved. We were given land, and rice, and this house. And every so often the guardian comes for one of us. Grandfather first, within days. My father the next year. My sister, Kanha, in 1920, in the water, while I held her hand and could do nothing.

The pact is bound by five seals. Break all five, and the one who is named may end it. That is what the old men told me, and the monks, and the manuscripts. I believe them. I have to.
Journal page B · Sok's desk drawer (A2-07) The first seal is the bronze token Grandfather wore. It is in the iron chest behind the planks of this room.

I broke the chest key into three and gave the pieces to the water jars beneath the house, where only family would look. Burn the token in the incense at the coffin, with matches blessed at our altar, before the third hour. Do not turn around while it burns. Whatever stands behind you, do not turn around.

Then the map. Then the pagoda. Then the rest. I am sorry it falls to you.

06Lore Notes L1–L5

L1 · Journal page · hall altar drawer Kanha used to make the betel tray for the ancestors every new moon. She drew it for the girl so she would learn. The girl never learned. She was twelve when we lost her mother, and Rith took her to the city, and that was that. I should have written to her more. I should have told her everything. I told her nothing.
L2 · Journal page · clay jar J3, under the house The clinic on the lake. I still dream of it. The water came up through the floor that night, as if something wanted her. I keep my medical logs from that year locked away. One day someone will need to know the date. The date matters.
L3 · Journal page · Sok's wardrobe Vuthy was five when his mother died. He used to sit under this house and line up stones and tell me they were soldiers guarding us. I sent him to her family in town because I could not bear to look at him and count the years. He thinks I didn't love him. He is all I love.
L4 · Land deed · storage shed Résidence de Battambang, March 1911. Certificate of concession: forty hectares of rice land granted to Monsieur Chhun, son of Chea, and his heirs. Stamped and signed. In the margin, in faded Khmer handwriting: "From nothing, in one year."
L5 · Photograph · storage shed A woman laughing on these same front stairs, a small girl on her lap holding a pencil. On the back, in Rith's handwriting: "Kanha and Sora, Battambang, 1913. She drew the house today."

09Translation and Review Notes

ItemNote
Forms of address"Yay" (grandmother), "cousin", and how Vuthy and Sora address each other must use correct Khmer kinship terms and politeness levels.
Blessing (E1-A1-09-3)Replace with an appropriate traditional phrase if the advisor prefers.

French deed (L4)Check the wording and format of a 1911 land concession.
SubtitlesMaximum two lines, about 42 characters per line in English; Khmer lines may need more time on screen.
